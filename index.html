About

I work at the intersection of optimization, machine learning, and applied spatial data science . My research focuses on understanding transportation systems and developing methods to support better decisions under uncertainty.

My doctoral research in Intelligent Transportation Systems at the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) combines reinforcement learning, network optimization, simulation, and behavioral modeling. I have held visiting research appointments at Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and KTH Royal Institute of Technology.

My background in applied data science builds on a Master of Urban Spatial Analytics at the University of Pennsylvania and research experience at The Wharton School. I integrate and analyze large spatial, mobility, satellite, sensor, and text datasets, combining data engineering, indicator construction, statistical modeling, and machine learning to investigate urban challenges and inform planning and policy decisions.

Research

Optimization & Machine Learning

Designing and controlling mobility systems through mathematical modeling and learning-based decision making.

  • Reinforcement learning and Bayesian optimization for dynamic tolling and demand management.
  • Hierarchical and multi-agent reinforcement learning for coordinated operational decisions.
  • Mathematical programming and network design for demand-responsive transport.
  • Autonomous fleet management with behavioral responses and endogenous congestion.

Applied Data Science

Turning heterogeneous real-world data into spatial insights, indicators, and predictive models.

  • Geospatial analysis of accessibility, neighborhood characteristics, and mobility inequities.
  • Statistical modeling, spatial feature engineering, and cross-validation.
  • Large-scale data integration and processing using Python, R, SQL, and cloud infrastructure.
  • Natural language processing for extracting structured indicators from text.

PhD Thesis

Design and Control of Future Mobility Systems: Temporal Networks, Autonomous Fleets, and Tradable Credits

PhD Thesis ↗ Defense Slides ↗

My PhD investigated how the characteristics of a decision problem should guide the choice of mathematical models and solution methods. Across demand-responsive transport design, autonomous fleet management, and day-to-day tolling, I developed mathematical models capturing interactions among operational decisions, user behavior, and system dynamics.
